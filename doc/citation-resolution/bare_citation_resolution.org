** Note on Schmidt

Schmidt is NOT primarily a Shakespeare editor — Alexander Schmidt's
major work is the /Shakespeare-Lexicon/ (a dictionary/concordance),
already showing up separately in our data under the "s.v." locator
pattern. The 73 bare "Schmidt:" citations most likely also default to
the Lexicon, but I flagged this as genuinely ambiguous in the earlier
pass and I'll repeat that flag here rather than resolve it by fiat —
it deserves a look at a handful of actual instances before deciding.

* What this changes about the resolution task

The earlier framing — "build a canonical-editions table" — undersold
how much of this cascade is actually Tier 0/Tier 2 work: pointing
citations back at data we already parsed, not new research. Only
Tier 1 needed real recall of 18th/19th-c. editing history, and only
Tier 4/5 are genuinely open questions. I'd suggest treating Tiers 0-3
as close to done, and spending actual review time on Tiers 4-5, which
total under 40 occurrences — a small, bounded pile of "ask Cliff"
rather than a research program.
